The first 20 days

The scenario

The request is rarely the real need

A business asks for a communication course for its managers. Twenty days of scoping shows that communication is not the problem.

New managers were promoted without being shown how to hold a probation conversation, and nobody owns the handover between two teams. A course would have been delivered, enjoyed and forgotten. The cause would still be there.

What scoping replaces

  • A traditional training needs analysis that asks people what course they would like.
  • Design that starts from a request and not from evidence.
  • Learning that is measured by attendance and not by what changes.
Day by day

Four stages, five days each

Every stage happens in your real environment, with your people. Strong relationships are built from the first conversation, and they are what make the findings honest.

Days 1 to 5

Listen

  • SEEN conversations at every level: CEO, leaders, managers and teams.
  • What does good look like here, and where does it break down?
  • Gather the data you already track.
  • Agree what success would mean for the business.
Days 6 to 10

Observe

  • Time on the floor on a busy day and a quiet day.
  • Briefings, one to ones, handovers and customer moments.
  • What is said compared with what is done.
  • Habits that help, and habits that hold talent back.
Days 11 to 15

Diagnose

  • Personalised diagnostics that give each person an archetype they recognise.
  • Skills scans against the standards of each role.
  • Results by person, by team and by level.
  • Strengths as well as gaps.
Days 16 to 20

Agree

  • A cross functional fishbone session to find the root cause.
  • Priorities ranked by impact and urgency.
  • The behaviours to build, and how they will be measured.
  • A blueprint presented and agreed before design begins.

A typical example. Twenty working days suits most businesses. Timescales flex with size, budget and how urgent each priority is.

SEEN

Five things SEEN brings to the surface

SEEN is our bespoke scoping method. It replaces the traditional training needs analysis with evidence from conversations, observation and data, so people feel seen and the business sees clearly.

1

Invisible pain points

The things everyone works around and nobody raises, because they have become normal.

2

Existing strengths

What already works and who already does it well. This is what we build on first.

3

Where learning needs building

The places where adult learning principles are missing, so training has not been sticking.

4

Blueprint behaviours

The specific behaviours and habits that would change performance if they became consistent.

5

Role standards

What good looks like in each role, written plainly, so design and measurement have something to stand on.

Observations

What people do on a busy day tells us more than any survey

Observation is where the real picture appears. It is done openly, with permission, and it looks for what is working as much as what is not.

  • How managers brief. Is the priority clear, and does the team leave knowing what good looks like today?
  • How decisions are made. Who decides, how quickly, and what happens when information is missing?
  • How feedback is given. In the moment, saved for later, or avoided?
  • How pressure is handled. Who stays clear, who rushes and who goes quiet?
  • How teams hand over. Where work passes between people is where most problems start.
What we look forThe habit behind the result, not the person to blame.
The fishbone

Root cause, found together

On day sixteen, people from different departments and levels sit at one table. The problem as it was first described goes at the head. Everything found in the first fifteen days is placed on the bones, and the group works back to the causes that matter most.

Why it works

The people closest to the work name the causes, so the solution is collective and it belongs to them. It separates a skills gap from a process gap or a culture gap, which a course alone would never fix.

What it changes

The brief. The business moves from asking for a course to agreeing two or three priorities that would shift performance, with the right people already behind them.

Two starting points

Partnering with your team, or building from scratch

Partnering with an existing team

  • Work alongside your internal people from the first day.
  • Build on what already works before adding anything new.
  • Upskill your trainers, managers and champions as we go.
  • Leave your team more resourceful and more autonomous.

Building from scratch

  • Foundations before volume: role standards and the behaviours that matter.
  • The first manager pathway and onboarding designed from evidence.
  • Simple measures the business already understands.
  • A proof of concept before anything is rolled out.
